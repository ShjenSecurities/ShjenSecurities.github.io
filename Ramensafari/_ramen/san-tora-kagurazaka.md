My first ramen in Japan. Upon arrival in Tokyo, I took my time getting acquainted to the famously intricate subway lines.

I walked through the smaller streets — alley ways — and stumbled upon a ramen shop with a long line in front of it. There were about 30 people waiting in a quiet queue. I knew then that I was in the right spot! After a short 30 minute wait I moved to the front where there were four seats by the front door, covered by banners. Shortly, a woman came to open the door, parting the tapestries, and beckoning me in. To the left of the front door is a small box similar to a vending machine awaiting input and displaying selections. Once paid, the host ushered me to my seat. About a seven minute wait and a bowl of steaming ramen arrived. The scent of Tonkotsu broth promising life and nourishment. The humble, white, undecorated bowl having a quiet perfection. The fish cakes were seared to golden perfection, sliced shallowly to provide an appealing visual texture. The marinated egg was delightful, the yolk so orange, it hovered on red.

As you leave everyone says, *Arigatou Gozaimasu.* — Thank you! (formal and polite)
