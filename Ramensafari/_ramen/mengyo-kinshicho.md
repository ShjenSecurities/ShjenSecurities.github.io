Exploring a new area of Tokyo today, NE direction. This is another peaceful and calm section of the metropolis — Sumida City. Along the main shopping district are these islands of greenery. In most of these parks are little shrines; however I have yet to observe any of the Japanese interest with them. Mostly they take short rests on the benches, enjoying the scenery in the moment. The ability to landscape plants on the buildings is a unique & beautiful integration. Down the street a hotel stands prominently, vibrant greenery covering the majority of the front & proliferating to each of the 11 balconies, including the roof. I'm envious, to say the least.

In discussions with the host, the topic of ramen quickly arose. I'm eager to follow their advice on my next ramen. The hotel is very traditional & historic — aged 100 years! The upstairs ryokan rooms are divided by wooden slabs filled in with paper-shoji. Definitely where the term "paper-thin walls" originated. As the host insisted, I headed out to find Madai Mengyo, a ramen shop specializing in fish-based broth. The quaint shop had 14 seats, featuring a lively open kitchen. The aroma of fish is readily apparent upon entry. To begin, you must make your choices on the vending machine — a process I'm becoming quite familiar with. I chose the special fish ramen with thick broth. The fish flavor was very intense, maybe a little too intense for me, but I slurped away with enthusiasm nonetheless. Just as advertised, the broth was thick & incredibly rich, the viscous liquid coating everything it touches. The sensation & flavor leaves a strong impression. Delicious & unique.
