I've contacted a talented tattoo artist who specializes in clean line work — Alan. She works at a humble shop near Shinjuku area in Tokyo, Konpaku Tattoo Shop. When you enter it has these beautiful Japanese paper dividers covered in abstract typography bars. A single vase rests on the floor within a white salmon cubby. A few small, square Japanese rectangular lamps make a pathway to the depths of the shop, each placed in front of an artist's divided space. It's beautiful, simplistic & clean. Alan is a tiny woman, measuring below five feet. I feel like I am towering over her at my 6'1" height. She wears these stylish tinted glasses with circular lenses & exudes a quiet confidence & artistry. For three hours I sit as she uses a wireless, single-needle tattoo gun, etching perfectly straight lines into the flesh of my skin. The design is a simple, geometric scene topped with a bowl of ramen — chopsticks holding wavy noodles in the air. It's perfect.

I worked up quite the appetite & Alan gave me directions to a nearby ramen shop she frequents. They specialize in Tsukemen style ramen where the broth arrives in a smaller bowl, served hot. The noodles come piled on a separate plate — cold. The thick plate combines warm accompaniments — soft egg, bean sprouts, cabbage & an intricately folded wonton. I dove right in with gusto, slipping some noodles into the broth — twice — and slurping them up. The broth & oil clung to them partially, the collision of cold noodles & warm broth a surprisingly delightful mixture on the palette. An entirely unique experience for me, changing my entire perspective on what qualifies as ramen. Absolutely delectable.
